[Doctor's Note](https://thymosin-report.hbsnzj.com/)

Inside your body, there is a system that helps control your temperature. When your core temperature is a bit high, your nervous system sends extra blood to places like your legs. This is called vasodilation, and it lets heat escape to keep you cool. Research shows that during heat stress, more blood flows into your muscles and skin, including in your legs. This extra blood can make your legs feel warm on the inside. In some cases, this feeling can also be related to conditions like restless leg syndrome where the nerves send mixed signals that make you feel extra warmth. Basically, the extra warmth is your body’s way of dealing with heat and trying to keep your body balanced. If your legs feel very hot or you have other signs like pain or swelling, it might be a good idea to talk with a healthcare provider so they can check your circulation and nerve signals. For many people, this process is a normal part of how the body regulates its temperature.
