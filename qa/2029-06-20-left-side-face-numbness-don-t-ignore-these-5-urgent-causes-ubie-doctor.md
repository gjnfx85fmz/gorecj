What are the causes of numbness on the left side of the face?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Left-side facial numbness typically stems from three main causes: trigeminal nerve dysfunction (which controls facial sensation), carotid artery problems that reduce blood flow to the face, or growths near the brain such as epidermoid cysts pressing on nerves.

Common causes of left-side facial numbness:

Carotid artery problems — reduced blood flow, dissection, or narrowing affecting the face

Brain growths — epidermoid cysts or tumors pressing on surrounding nerves

Because these causes range from manageable to serious, identifying the source early matters. [Side Face Numbness](https://beautybiometrics.hbsnzj.com/blog/8345537869.html)

Facial numbness, especially on one side, can be due to several reasons. One common cause is problems with the trigeminal nerve, which is responsible for sensation in the face. When this nerve is affected, it can lead to numbness or tingling sensations.

Another possible cause is issues with the carotid artery, which supplies blood to the face and brain. If there's a problem with this artery, it can affect the nerves and lead to numbness.

Additionally, growths near the brain, such as epidermoid cysts located in the cerebellopontine angle, can press on nerves and cause facial numbness. These cysts are non-cancerous but can still cause symptoms by putting pressure on surrounding structures.
