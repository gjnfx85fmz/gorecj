What to Consider

Fabric

Most rugby shirts are typically made from cotton—but not all cotton is created equal. The best versions use heavyweight cotton jersey, which feels substantial without being stiff. A thicker knit gives that structured look rugby shirts are known for, while brushed or washed-down finishes add softness and that lived-in feel from day one. Garment-washed fabrics are especially great because they resist shrinking and only get softer over time.

Fit

A good rugby shirt should fit like your favorite sweatshirt—relaxed but not sloppy. You want enough room though the chest and shoulder to move easily, with sleeves that hit right at the wrist. The hem should land around mid-fly so it looks polished whether you wear it untucked with jeans or tucked into chinos for a more dressier look.

Construction

Even if you’re not a fabric nerd, there are a few different ways to tell if a rugby shirt is made well:

Thick seams

Give the shirt a quick tug—if the seams feel tight and don’t pull apart, that’s a good sign it’ll hold its shape.

Collar structure

The collar should stand up on its own instead of going limp after just one wash.

Cuffs and placket

These areas get the most wear, so look for fabric that feels reinforced or slightly denser there—it means it’ll last longer.

Button stitching

Loose threads near the buttons are usually a red flag for poor quality.

Style Details

Rugby shirts are known for their horizontal stripes, but not every version has to look like you’re heading to a college tailgate. Many modern brands are remixing the classic—think solid bodies with contrast collars, subtle color-blocking, or thinner stripes that still feel fresh. Our favorite picks below strike that balance perfectly: easy to wear, not too flashy, and built with just enough personality to stand out in your weekly rotation.
