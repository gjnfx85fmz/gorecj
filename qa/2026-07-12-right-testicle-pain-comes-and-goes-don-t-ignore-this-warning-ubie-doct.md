Intermittent right testicle pain can result from several conditions, ranging from common to rare. Possible causes include chronic pelvic pain syndrome, varicocele, epididymitis, testicular torsion (intermittent), inguinal hernia, or rare conditions like polyorchidism (having more than two testicles). Pain may be sharp, dull, or radiating, and can occur with or without swelling.

Because testicle pain can occasionally signal a serious issue requiring urgent care, it's important not to guess. Explanation

Right testicle pain that comes and goes can be related to several conditions. According to available references, it can be a symptom of chronic pelvic pain. This type of pain can occur when muscles in the pelvic floor are not working well. Another possibility is polyorchidism, which means having more than two testicles, though this is very rare.

What You Should Know:

Chronic Pelvic Pain: This can cause testicular pain that changes in intensity. The pain might be due to muscle issues in the pelvic area.

Polyorchidism: This rare condition involves having extra testicles and can cause pain. It's not common, so it might not be the cause for most people.
