Introduction / Key Takeaways

A NAD⁺ shot involves the delivery of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD⁺) directly into the bloodstream—typically via an intravenous (IV) or intramuscular injection. NAD⁺ is a fundamental coenzyme found in every cell, where it plays an essential role in cellular energy production, metabolism, and DNA repair. What is NAD⁺?

NAD⁺ is a naturally occurring coenzyme present in all living cells. It functions as the oxidized form of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide and plays a central role in redox reactions (where it alternates between NAD⁺ and its reduced form NADH). In these reactions, NAD⁺ accepts electrons and becomes NADH, which then donates electrons in the mitochondrial electron transport chain to help generate ATP, the body’s primary energy molecule. [1]

Beyond energy generation, NAD⁺ is vital for mitochondrial maintenance, DNA repair via PARP (poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase) and sirtuin pathways, chromatin regulation, and adaptive responses to cellular stress. [2]

Importantly, NAD⁺ levels tend to decline with age, chronic stress, poor sleep, and metabolic dysregulation—leading to reduced mitochondrial function, increased oxidative damage, and impaired repair capacity. [3]

Differences between NAD⁺, NAD, and NADH

Our article, What Is The Difference Between NAD, NAD+, And NADH?, offers an indepth look on this topic. In short:

NAD⁺: The oxidized form of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide; serves as the electron acceptor.

NADH: The reduced form; carries electrons to be used in ATP generation.

NAD (sometimes used loosely): May refer generically to the family of NAD-related molecules (NAD⁺, NADH, NADP, NADPH) but in the context of therapy usually “NAD” is shorthand for NAD⁺.

Understanding the ratio of NAD⁺ to NADH is important because a higher NAD⁺/NADH ratio is associated with more robust cellular metabolism and repair, while a low ratio is often found in aged or dysfunctional tissues.

What is a NAD⁺ Shot?

A NAD⁺ shot refers to the administration of NAD⁺ via injection—either intramuscularly (IM) or intravenously (IV)—so that the molecule enters the bloodstream directly rather than being absorbed through the digestive tract. This method is chosen because it offers faster and more predictable delivery, higher bioavailability, and bypasses first-pass metabolism in the gut.

When you receive a NAD⁺ shot, the intent is to elevate NAD⁺ levels in circulation, thereby giving cells greater access to this coenzyme for mitochondrial energy production, repair processes, and signal-transduction activities. The elevated NAD⁺ may support enhanced cellular energy, improved cognitive focus, faster recovery, and healthier metabolic function.

2. Support Brain Health & Focus

Because neurons are highly energy-dependent, NAD⁺ plays a critical role in brain-cell mitochondrial health. Depleted NAD⁺ is associated with neurodegenerative changes. [5]

By elevating NAD⁺, there is potential for improved mental clarity, focus, and mood stability. Some wellness protocols integrate NAD⁺ shots specifically for “brain fog,” cognitive support, and recovery from stress.

While large human trials are still limited, pre-clinical data support NAD⁺ in ameliorating neuroinflammation and mitochondrial damage in brain cells. [6]

3. Recovery & Cellular Repair

NAD⁺ is a substrate for DNA repair enzymes (e.g., PARP) and sirtuins that manage cellular stress responses, autophagy, and mitochondrial turnover. [7]

In recovery settings—such as post-exercise, illness or intensive training—higher NAD⁺ may support faster tissue repair, decreased inflammation, and improved recovery outcome.

4. Anti-Aging & Longevity

NAD⁺ depletion is now considered one hallmark of aging—a molecular feature associated with mitochondrial decline, genomic instability, and reduced repair capacity.

Because NAD⁺ supports sirtuin activation (which regulates cell survival, metabolism and lifespan in certain models), elevating NAD⁺ is theoretically linked to healthier aging. [8]

However, while preliminary trials show promise, it’s important to emphasise: human clinical evidence for NAD⁺ shots as a definitive “anti-aging” therapy is still emerging.

Important Considerations & Safety

NAD⁺ shots should be administered by a licensed medical provider in a clinical or properly supervised setting.

These injections are not a replacement for a healthy lifestyle. Their effectiveness will be limited without foundational habits: healthy nutrition, exercise, sleep, and stress management.

Individual results vary depending on age, baseline health, genetic factors, lifestyle, and the frequency/dose of treatment.

Though NAD⁺ supplementation appears safe in many studies, long-term human data are limited, and there may be unknown risks (for example in cancer-related pathways) when manipulating NAD⁺ metabolism. [9]
