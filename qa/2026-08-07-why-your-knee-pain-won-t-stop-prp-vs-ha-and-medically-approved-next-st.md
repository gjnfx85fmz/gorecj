Why Your Knee Pain Won't Stop? PRP vs HA for Knees and Medically Approved Next Steps

Persistent knee pain can be frustrating and limit daily activities. Whether it's a dull ache when climbing stairs or sharp pain during exercise, understanding why your knee hurts and exploring the right treatments is crucial. Two popular injectable options—platelet-rich plasma (PRP) and hyaluronic acid (HA)—often come up in discussions. Here, we'll break down common causes, compare PRP vs HA for knees, review medically approved next steps, and help you decide what to try next.

What Keeps Knee Pain Lingering?

Knee pain can continue for months or years if the underlying issue isn't fully addressed. Common reasons include: • Osteoarthritis: Gradual wear‐and‐tear of cartilage• Meniscus tears: Degenerative or acute cartilage injuries• Ligament injuries: ACL, MCL, or other sprains that never fully heal• Patellofemoral pain: "Runner's knee" from overuse or alignment issues• Bursitis or tendinitis: Inflammation of fluid‐filled sacs or tendons• Mechanical problems: Loose fragments, misalignment, or instability

Without proper diagnosis and targeted treatment, pain signals persist, leading to stiffness, swelling, and muscle weakness.

Getting a Proper Diagnosis

Before comparing PRP vs HA for knees, you need a clear diagnosis. A physician or orthopedist will typically:

Take a detailed history (onset, triggers, prior treatments)

Perform a physical exam (range of motion, swelling, tenderness)

Order imaging if needed (X-ray for arthritis, MRI for soft tissue)

Accurate diagnosis guides you to the most effective therapies and prevents wasted time and money.

Conservative Treatments First

Most knee pain should start with non-invasive approaches: • Rest and activity modification – avoid high-impact sports• Physical therapy – strengthen muscles, improve flexibility• Weight management – reduce joint stress• Bracing or taping – stabilize patella and control tracking• Over-the-counter pain relievers – NSAIDs like ibuprofen

If these measures fail after 6–12 weeks, your doctor may discuss injection therapies such as PRP or HA.

What Are PRP and HA Injections?

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP)• Your blood is drawn and spun down to concentrate platelets.• Platelets release growth factors that may promote tissue healing.• Injected into the knee joint or around damaged tendons/ligaments.

Hyaluronic Acid (HA)• A natural lubricant found in joint fluid.• Injection aims to restore cushioning and improve joint glide.• Often marketed as "viscosupplementation."

Comparing PRP vs HA for Knees

Effectiveness• PRP: Studies show mixed results. Some patients report up to 60% pain relief lasting 6–12 months, especially in mild to moderate osteoarthritis.• HA: Benefits tend to be modest, with pain relief lasting 3–6 months. More effective in early arthritis than advanced cases.

Onset of Relief• PRP: May take 4–6 weeks for full effect as healing processes ramp up.• HA: Relief often starts within 1–2 weeks, peaking around 4–8 weeks.

Safety Profile• PRP: Uses your own blood—low risk of allergic reaction or infection when performed under sterile conditions.• HA: Generally well tolerated. Rarely, local swelling or redness at the injection site.

Number of Treatments• PRP: Usually 1–3 injections spaced weeks apart.• HA: Typical course is 3–5 weekly injections, depending on the product.

Pros and Cons of PRP

Pros• Utilizes your body's own healing factors• Potential for longer-lasting relief in some patients• Low risk of allergic reaction

Cons• Inconsistent results—some see little benefit• Higher upfront cost• Multiple blood draws and processing steps

Pros and Cons of HA

Pros• Faster onset of symptom relief• Well‐studied with predictable safety profile• May be covered by insurance

Cons• Effects may be shorter-lived (3–6 months)• Less potential for tissue repair• Multiple visits required

Who Might Benefit Most?

PRP may be a good choice if you:• Are relatively young (<60) with early osteoarthritis• Have failed HA or other conservative treatments• Want to avoid surgery and systemic medications• Can handle the cost and downtime

HA injections may suit you if you:• Prefer a treatment with predictable, moderate relief• Have mild to moderate osteoarthritis• Seek quicker symptom relief• Need insurance-covered or lower-cost options

Other Advanced Options

If both PRP and HA fall short, consider:• Corticosteroid injections – powerful anti-inflammatory effects (temporary relief)• Geniculate nerve block or radiofrequency ablation – targets nerve pain signals• Stem cell therapies – still investigational, limited insurance coverage• Arthroscopic surgery – removal of loose fragments or repair of meniscus tears• Partial or total knee replacement – for severe, end-stage arthritis

Self-Care Strategies for All Stages

• Continue physical therapy or home exercises to maintain strength• Use ice or heat as guided by your therapist or doctor• Maintain a healthy weight and low-impact activities (swimming, cycling)• Wear supportive shoes and consider orthotics if you have alignment issues• Listen to your body—avoid pushing through sharp pain

When to Seek Urgent Medical Attention

While most knee pain isn't life threatening, contact a doctor right away if you experience:• Severe swelling, warmth, and redness (possible infection)• Fever above 100.4°F with knee pain• Inability to bear any weight on the leg• Sudden deformity, popping sound, or suspected major ligament tear

Speak to a doctor about anything that could be serious or life threatening.
