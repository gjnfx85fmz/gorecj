Why do I get dizzy multiple times a day with Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV)?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) can trigger dizziness several times a day when tiny calcium crystals in the inner ear become dislodged and disrupt your balance signals. It's one of the most common causes of vertigo and typically flares up with head movements, such as rolling over in bed, looking up, or bending down.

Because frequent dizziness can also point to other conditions—like Meniere's disease, vestibular migraine, or low blood pressure—it's important to identify the likely cause before deciding on next steps. Explanation

Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) is a common cause of dizziness. It happens when tiny crystals in your ear move into areas they shouldn't be, which affects your balance. These crystals can cause your brain to receive confusing messages about your body's position, leading to dizziness.

Why Does It Happen?

Movement: When you move your head in certain ways, like looking up or rolling over in bed, these crystals shift and cause dizziness.

Frequency: Episodes can happen multiple times a day, especially with specific head movements.
