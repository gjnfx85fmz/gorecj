Emergency contraception

More commonly called the “morning-after pill,” emergency contraception works by temporarily stopping your ovaries from releasing an egg. Taking the morning-after pill can effectively avoid unwanted pregnancies, whether the condom broke or you had an unavoidable birth control lapse.

You must take emergency contraception within 3-5 days after having unprotected sex, depending on the brand, so time is of the essence. We recommend placing your order as soon as possible (as in the “morning after”) to ensure your pill arrives in time. Nurx has a generally excellent track record with their convenient, expedited shipping, and our testers received their orders in 1-3 business days.

You have two choices in terms of brands: Ella and Plan B (as generic New Day). If you are in a rush and don’t have time to do your research, we recommend going with Ella. This is because it works up to 120 hours (five days) after unprotected sex and works equally well on day one or five. New Day is another solid option, but it doesn’t work as well on people who weigh more than 155 pounds, and its effectiveness wanes dramatically after 72 hours.4

PrEP

Truvada, often known as PrEP (for its status as preexposure prophylaxis), is a medication you can take to significantly reduce your likelihood of contracting HIV. In fact, daily PrEP use can reduce your sexually-transmitted risk by up to 99% and can drop your risk of getting HIV through shared needles by up to 70%.5 It’s important to note that PrEP won’t keep you from getting other sexually transmitted infections, so you should still use a condom or other prevention methods alongside PrEP. PrEP is also not appropriate for those who are currently HIV-positive; it won’t keep you from sharing it with a potential partner. However, many providers recommend taking PrEP if you’re at high risk.

Nurx knows how important proper care is since there still is no cure for HIV. Though you can get HIV through several different avenues, you’ll still need to take an STI home test first. There are two different types of tests you can take:

Comprehensive, STI and HIV test: $164

Basic, HIV test: $94

Both tests look for your HIV status, hepatitis B status, creatinine (kidney function) levels, and pregnancy (if applicable). A study presented in 2020 (but is yet unpublished in a clinical journal) found that PrEP might be able to prevent hepatitis B.6 PrEP is known to help treat hepatitis B as long as you don’t have any liver or kidney problems, so there’s no need to worry about not getting a prescription if you test positive.7 However, PrEP does increase your creatinine levels, so it’s not a good option if you have kidney disease (hence the creatinine test on this panel).8

The STI test also looks for gonorrhea, chlamydia, and syphilis. No matter which you choose, both versions of the HIV test ask for finger-prick blood samples you can perform at home. They’re almost completely painless and take less than two minutes to complete. In our opinion, Nurx is the better of the two because they offer both an HIV-only and an HIV and STI test to help you prepare for PrEP. (PlushCare only offers HIV-only.)

Nurx only sends their PrEP packs in three-month increments, so it’s easy to remember that you need to redo your lab work. Nurx will contact you to see if you want to continue the prescription before they send a lab test.

PrEP is almost always covered by insurance, but if yours doesn’t cover it (or you don’t have or can’t use insurance), Nurx connects you with several payment assistance programs to subsidize the cost. HPV screening

According to the CDC, 75% of sexually active adults have had HPV.11 While there are over 150 strains, screening for this virus is fortunately very easy.

Nurx offers an HPV Test Kit that allows people with vaginas to screen themselves for HPV at home. Regular HPV tests are suggested for women 25-29 and recommended for women 30-65. After a few days, someone from Nurx’s medical team will reach out with your results.

FAQ about Nurx

How does Nurx show up on a bank statement?

Some online birth control companies will use a discreet company name that’s unrecognizable when billing. Nurx, however, doesn’t. All transactions will appear from “Nurx.co” and the amount you paid on your bank statement. There won’t be any information about what you’ve bought or why, but be aware that there’s no way to keep your purchase 100% private.

How can I order birth control online with complete confidentiality?

No privacy is ever fully guaranteed, especially on the Internet. However, if you need birth control but aren’t in a safe situation (such as an abusive relationship), there are some tips you can use to gain more privacy.

If you’re worried about your insurance (or those who pay for it) finding out, Nurx recommends that you pay out-of-pocket. However, be aware that your purchase will still appear from “Nurx.co” on your bank statement. You can request that your insurance keeps your sensitive health information private (such as payments related to birth control or STI testing). That said, not every insurance company is willing to hide disclosures (unless you live in California, where they’re legally mandated to do so).

Using secure Internet habits will also help to increase your confidentiality. This means implementing extra security steps like making a new email address that no one else knows about for your Nurx account, signing up with a secure password, and using a VPN to hide your IP address. You can also have Nurx deliver your birth control to a different trusted address if you worry about someone else intercepting or opening the package.

Does Nurx employ real doctors?

Every medical professional that Nurx employs is licensed to practice. Their practices range from medical doctors (MD) and nurse practitioners (NP) to doctors of osteopathic medicine (DO) and physician’s assistants (PA). They come from diverse backgrounds to provide the wide range of care in the states where Nurx operates, and all of the 79 medical professionals Nurx employs are well-educated, certified, and licensed. Nurx also has a three-doctor medical advisor team covering migraine, mental health, and dermatologic care.

At any point, you can look up a Nurx professional’s credentials through their National Provider Identifier (NPI) numbers or by Googling their name and state to find their licensure. This ensures that you can do your own research to verify that everything Nurx says is true, providing peace of mind.

How does Nurx give prescriptions?

Like going to your primary care provider in person, you'll meet briefly with a member of Nurx's care team licensed in your state. This can be done asynchronously over text through Nurx's platform while they read the medical questionnaire you filled out, but some states require video calls. Either way, after your provider has looked at your history, they'll determine whether or not it's safe for you to take birth control, an antidepressant, or another kind of medication and write a prescription for you just like any other doctor.

Does Nurx accept Medicare?

At this time, Nurx doesn’t take government-provided insurance, including Medicaid and Medicare. Luckily, they’ve made their out-of-pocket costs as low as possible to accommodate people who can’t use their insurance (or don’t want to).

Can Nurx prescribe Adderall (or other ADHD medications)?

There are some mental health medications that Nurx can prescribe, but controlled substances like stimulants (such as Adderall, Vyvanse, or Ritalin) and benzodiazepines (such as Xanax, Valium, and Ativan) aren’t on that list. This is because laws around controlled substances require strict monitoring from a licensed healthcare professional beyond the scope of what a service like Nurx provides. However, you’ll still be able to choose from 17 different mental health medications, including SSRIs, SNRIs, tricyclic antidepressants, beta-blockers, and other anxiety medications like hydroxyzine.

Can I transfer a current prescription to Nurx?

Not directly. There’s no way to have your current provider send your prescription to Nurx for them to fill and manage. If you’re currently taking medication or have found success in the past, when you go through your consultation with a Nurx provider, let them know your preference. They’ll re-prescribe it to you if it’s still a good fit.
