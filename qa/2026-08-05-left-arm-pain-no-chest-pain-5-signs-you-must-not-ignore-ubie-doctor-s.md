Left arm pain without chest pain is often caused by musculoskeletal or nerve-related issues rather than heart problems. Common culprits include thoracic outlet syndrome, cervical radiculopathy (a pinched nerve in the neck), nerve entrapment, rotator cuff injuries, or muscle strain. That said, heart-related conditions like angina or a heart attack can occasionally present with left arm pain alone, without chest discomfort—especially in women, older adults, and people with diabetes.

Explanation

When you experience pain in your left arm without any accompanying chest pain, it can point to a non-cardiac cause. One possible reason, although uncommon, is thoracic outlet syndrome, particularly related to the pectoralis minor, where nerves or blood vessels get compressed as they travel from the neck into the arm. This compression can lead to pain, numbness, or tingling in the arm. Another common cause is cervical radiculopathy, which happens when a nerve in the neck becomes irritated or compressed, and the pain radiates down the shoulder and arm. Sometimes, nerve entrapment in the shoulder or upper arm may be responsible. These conditions are different from cardiac issues, which typically cause chest pain and may radiate into the arm, but usually come with additional symptoms. It is important to discuss these symptoms with your healthcare provider, who can perform a physical exam and possibly order imaging studies to determine the exact cause, so they can recommend the best treatment plan for you. To help identify what might be causing your arm pain, you can take a free symptom checker assessment before your appointment. It is also important to know that left arm pain without chest pain does not rule out a heart problem and you seek immediate medical evaluation.
