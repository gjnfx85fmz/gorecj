Narcolepsy is a chronic neurological sleep disorder that typically begins with three hallmark symptoms: sudden, uncontrollable sleep attacks, excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS), and cataplexy—brief episodes of muscle weakness often triggered by strong emotions like laughter or surprise. Other early signs may include sleep paralysis, vivid hallucinations when falling asleep, and disrupted nighttime sleep.

Because these symptoms often overlap with other conditions like sleep apnea, depression, or chronic fatigue, narcolepsy is frequently misdiagnosed or overlooked for years. Recognizing the pattern early is key to getting the right treatment and improving quality of life. Narcolepsy is a sleep disorder that can cause sudden sleepiness and sleep attacks. People with narcolepsy may feel very sleepy during the day and might suddenly fall asleep at any time. One key sign is cataplexy, which is when someone experiences sudden muscle weakness, often triggered by strong emotions like laughter or surprise.

[Narcolepsy Warning Signs](https://memorycircuits.hbsnzj.com/)

Early Warning Signs:

Excessive Daytime Sleepiness: Feeling very sleepy during the day, even after a good night's sleep.

Sudden Sleep Attacks: Falling asleep suddenly and without warning.

Cataplexy: Sudden loss of muscle strength, which can lead to slurred speech or weakness in the arms and legs.

Sleep Paralysis: Being unable to move or speak when falling asleep or waking up.

Hallucinations: Seeing or hearing things that aren't there when falling asleep or waking up.
