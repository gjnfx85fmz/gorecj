Bodybuilder and current trainer on the reboot of The Biggest Loser Steve Cook needed a challenge—so he decided to go back to his football roots. Cook was a three-year starter at linebacker for Dixie State University, where he says he weighed in at about 235 pounds at his largest.

"I was probably a little bit too big," he says. "I was that bodybuilder that also played football."

He went back to his alma mater's campus to see if he still has the physical tools to compete by taking on some of the tests from the NFL Combine's workout, which coaches at every level at football typically use to assess players.

"We got our work cut out for us today. I'm 35 years old, and I'm about 20 pounds less than what I was when I played. But I still got that quickness and I still got that explosiveness," he says.

"Ah, completely died there. Didn't get as many reps as I wanted, but I'll take it," he says.

He heads to the university's Human Performance Center with the Science of Sport team, where he undergoes analysis for his vertical jump. After a couple tries, he hits 121 inches (about 10'1"), and his reach is 92".

"This is driving me nuts," says Cook.

Then he heads outside to do incline sprints, and clocks in at 4.34.

"I may be faster than the average Dixie State athlete," he says, after he was told no athlete has beaten 6 seconds.

The next day, they hit the football field to run through drills.

First he does the pro agility test, and clocks in at 4.19 seconds, followed by "the L" lateral sprints with a time of 4.5 seconds. Notably, Cook skipped the 40 yard dash, typically the top test of speed for a football prospect. He says he ran a 4.71 at a park the week before.

Next, he's on to the long jump, and measures at about 9'3".

"We'll take it," he says. "All I know is I can already feel myself wearing that red again."

From there, he goes to catching balls spit out from a machine, and also sees how far he can throw the pigskin. His throw is 55 yards.
