Who are greens powders for?

Mostly composed of botanicals dried at low temperatures — including fruits, leafy green vegetables, and grasses — greens powders have come a long way from the wheatgrass shots of yesteryear.

Greens powders can be a great source of whole-food-based nutrients for almost anyone who’s interested in trying them — from athletes and parents to students, people on the go, or whoever else wants to increase their daily intake of fresh fruits and vegetables. In addition to fruits and veggies, these supplemental mixes often supply vitamins, minerals, antioxidants, and, in many cases, probiotics and digestive enzymes.

For athletes who are looking to avoid the substances banned by major sports organizations, some greens powders are specifically analyzed to guarantee they’re clean. In the case of Athletic Greens’ AG1, it’s NSF Certified for Sport (which means it’s tested for those substances, among other things).3

Also, there are a multitude of greens powders on the market that can fit almost any budget. While AG1 costs about $100 as a one-time purchase, our choice for best budget pick, Orgain Organic Superfoods + Probiotics, costs around $15 on the company’s Amazon store.

Who shouldn’t use greens powders?

As with any supplement, it’s a good idea to talk with your doctor before adding greens powders to your diet. But, if you take prescription medications or other supplements, it’s especially important you speak with your doctor to ensure that the greens powder you choose won’t interfere or give you too much of a certain nutrient. This is especially important if your greens powder offers more than 100% of your Daily Value of a vitamin or mineral; certain nutrients, like vitamin A or iron, among others, can potentially lead to serious side effects if you consume too much.7

Children, and those who are pregnant or nursing, shouldn’t consume greens powders — the nutrient levels may be too high for children, leading to toxicity, and some plant or herbal ingredients commonly found in greens powders can affect an unborn baby or pass into breast milk.8

Additionally, certain ingredients in greens powders can interact with medications or worsen medical conditions. Bromelain comes from the pineapple plant and can interact with certain medications, such as blood thinners, antibiotics, and sedatives.9 Some vegetables, like spinach and kale, contain vitamin K, which can also interfere with blood medications, and it should also be avoided by individuals with chronic kidney disease (CKD).10 We delve further into the safety concerns of greens powders in the section below.

And, finally, some greens powders can contain allergens:

AG1 contains soy.

Amazing Grass is technically allergen-free but is made in a facility that processes nuts.

Bloom’s coconut variety contains “natural coconut flavor” which may trigger a reaction in those who are allergic to it.

Are greens powders safe?

Most greens powders are generally safe for adults 18 and older who aren’t pregnant or breastfeeding. However, as we’ve said before, we always recommend speaking with your doctor first to make sure greens powders are a safe choice for you, especially if you have a chronic condition or take any prescription medication.

The following list notes some (but not all) potential risks or contraindications of greens powders.

Some common greens ingredients, like spirulina, chlorella, alfalfa, and echinacea may aggravate certain autoimmune conditions, such as lupus erythematosus, dermatomyositis, and pemphigus.4

Supplementing with vitamins A, E, and K should be avoided by those with chronic kidney disease (CKD).10

Ginkgo biloba and vitamin E can thin the blood, so they should be limited by those already on blood-thinning medications, like warfarin or aspirin.11

Ashwagandha may increase the effects of benzodiazepines, such as Valium and Xanax.12

With greens powders containing a wide range of ingredients (vegetables, herbs, adaptogens, and more), the above list only contains a few of the potential risks and interactions. This is why speaking with a doctor first is ideal.

Besides medications and medical conditions, there are other important details to look at when considering the safety of a dietary supplement — greens powders included. Below, we’ll look at some of the factors to keep in mind when purchasing a greens powder and how each issue might relate to you.

Greens powders vs. multivitamins

In an ideal situation, you’d obtain all of your essential nutrients from your diet, meaning that your food would cover the USDA's recommendation of:

1.5-2.5 cups of fruits20

2-4 cups of vegetables21

5-7oz equivalent of protein22

But even the most health-conscious among us often miss those marks in the hustle and bustle of a busy lifestyle, so supplementation may be necessary. Multivitamins and greens powders can both provide nutrients to fill in the gaps. So, how are they different, and which is better for you?

Multivitamins often include synthetic forms of vitamins and minerals, while greens powders are made from dried whole fruits, vegetables, and other botanicals. However, synthetic doesn’t necessarily mean that the nutrients in a multivitamin are somehow worse — research suggests that the bioavailability between synthetic and natural nutrients is about the same.23 24 While bioavailability is similar, there are still some minor differences. In one study on synthetic and natural vitamin B, for instance, the former didn’t demonstrate the same positive effect on oxidative stress as the latter.24

Additionally, all of the greens powders featured in our top picks supply probiotics that can assist in maintaining a healthy gut, whereas you’d likely have to take an additional probiotic supplement along with your multivitamin to get the same.

With more ingredients and different processing methods, greens powders do tend to cost more than daily multivitamins. And you’ll likely have to drink your greens, while you can often just chew a multivitamin gummy or swallow a pill in a sip or two of water. (Amazing Grass does offer a greens capsule, but you’ll need to take five of those per day.)

No matter which option you ultimately choose, we recommend maintaining a healthy diet as the primary source of your daily nutrients — even when taking a multivitamin or greens powder. Common ingredients in greens powders

While ingredients and ratios vary from brand to brand, greens powders commonly contain a mixture of nutritious green grasses, fruits, and vegetables; some supply additional digestive enzymes, probiotics, and herbal extracts. Below, we'll look at the science behind many common ingredients in greens powders and how they may benefit your physical well-being.

Spirulina

Spirulina, a blue-green microalgae, is one of the most popular ingredients in greens powders due to its rich antioxidant and nutrient profile.25

While more research is needed on its potential for helping humans, spirulina may be able to help support the immune system, act as a source of protein, protect against allergic reactions, reduce oral cancer risk in those who chew tobacco, and more.26

Wheatgrass

[Powder Our Top](https://weight-signal.sunsimiaosd.com/)

Wheatgrass is the fresh, newly sprouted leaves of the common wheat plant. It has been at the forefront of the health supplements market since it was first discovered and sold as a nutrient powerhouse in the 1930s and 40s by agricultural chemist Charles F. Schnabel.27

Wheatgrass possesses antioxidant properties, phytochemicals, vitamins, and active enzymes that the body easily absorbs, offering several potential health benefits, such as free radical scavenging.28

Barley grass

Barley grass is rich in gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), lutonarin (an antioxidant), superoxide dismutase (an enzyme), vitamins A, B1, C, and E, dietary fiber, polyphenols, and more.29

Barley grass has demonstrated antidiabetic, antioxidant, anticancer, hypolipidemic, and anti-inflammatory properties in research. It may help regulate blood pressure, promote better sleep, improve digestive function, and more.29

Chlorella

Chlorella is a type of freshwater algae with nutrients that can be difficult to absorb without proper preparation due to its hard cell wall — this is why you’ll often see “cracked cell wall” or “broken cell” or something similar beside it in the ingredients list.30

Commercially available forms of this algae “contain all the vitamins required by humans.” Chlorella has also demonstrated antioxidant, antidiabetic, hepatoprotective, and immunomodulatory effects (among others) in studies.30

Broccoli

[Our Top](https://innate-signal.jcesqc8.com/blog/9484814583.html)

This cruciferous vegetable can offer a multitude of benefits. It’s rich in vitamins A, C, and K, and it contains minerals like potassium, iron, and calcium. As a good source of dietary fiber, broccoli can help digestion and make you feel fuller for longer. Researchers also suggest that broccoli may help lower cholesterol, promote healthy blood pressure, improve heart function, support eye function, and boost the immune system.31

Spinach

Spinach is often called a “functional food,” providing vitamins A, C, and K, along with minerals like iron, potassium, and folate. Research suggests that the phytochemicals from spinach may be able to help prevent macromolecular oxidative damage, reduce inflammation, and fight cravings.32

Prebiotics and probiotics

While not all greens powders contain pre- and probiotics, all of our top picks do. Prebiotics, like inulin,33 are indigestible and act as a food source for the microflora in your gut;34 they can improve the balance between good and bad bacteria.35

[Our Top in](https://aegispeptide.snaketongueofficial.com/blog/9549828515.html)

Probiotics are live microorganisms that occur naturally in fermented foods like yogurt, kimchi, and sauerkraut. Probiotics maintain or improve the balance of gut bacteria and have shown promise for boosting immune function and digestive health.36 37 The two main genuses (or genera) of good bacteria are Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium.34

Digestive enzymes

Many greens powders include digestive enzymes among their ingredients. Astragalus, amylase, cellulase, protease, lactase, bromelain, and others can assist in digestion, helping break down proteins, carbohydrates, and lipids. These can be especially helpful for those with digestive or malabsorption disorders, like lactose intolerance.38

Adaptogens

Adaptogens refer to certain herbs, roots, and mushrooms that may help with stress relief and restoring homeostasis (they might help you “adapt” during a stressful situation).39 Common adaptogens included in greens powders include ashwagandha, ginseng, and astragalus. Researchers continue to study adaptogens for their potential benefits in treating stress and fatigue.40

Superfoods aren’t an officially recognized type or category of foods, but they generally include natural foods that are particularly nutritious, while being low in calories.
