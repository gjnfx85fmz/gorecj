Internal Medicine

Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) work best when taken 30–60 minutes before a meal. This timing activates the medication just as your stomach begins producing acid, allowing the PPI to block acid-producing pumps more effectively. Taking a PPI on an empty stomach before eating maximizes its ability to reduce acid, relieve heartburn, and heal conditions like GERD or ulcers.

Explanation

Taking a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) before meals is important for effective acid suppression. PPIs work by reducing the amount of acid your stomach makes. According to the research, PPIs are more effective when taken before a meal because they need to be in your system when your stomach starts to make acid in response to eating.

Why Take Before Meals?

Better Timing: Taking PPIs before meals ensures the medicine is in your body when your stomach is most active in producing acid.

Improved Effectiveness: Studies show that PPIs can suppress acid better if taken before eating, compared to taking them at other times.
