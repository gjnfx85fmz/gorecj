Naproxen Over 65: Protecting Your Stomach & Kidneys

Naproxen is a common pain reliever used for arthritis, back pain, gout flares, headaches, and other inflammatory conditions. It belongs to a group of medicines called nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs). For many adults, naproxen can be effective and convenient. However, if you are over 65, your stomach and kidneys need extra protection when using this medication.

This guide explains how naproxen works, why age matters, and what practical steps can help reduce risks—without causing unnecessary worry. It also highlights when it's important to speak to a doctor.

How Naproxen Works

Naproxen reduces pain and inflammation by blocking enzymes (COX-1 and COX-2) that produce prostaglandins. Prostaglandins play a role in:

Pain and swelling

Protecting the stomach lining

Supporting blood flow to the kidneys

When prostaglandins are reduced, pain improves—but protective effects in the stomach and kidneys may also decrease. This is why side effects are more likely with age or long-term use.

Why Age Over 65 Matters

As we age, the body processes medications differently. According to widely accepted medical guidance (including FDA labeling and geriatric prescribing standards), adults over 65 are more sensitive to NSAIDs like naproxen because:

The stomach lining becomes thinner and more fragile

Kidney function naturally declines with age

Many older adults take multiple medications that can interact

Recovery from side effects may take longer

This does not mean naproxen is off-limits, but it does mean it should be used thoughtfully.

Stomach Risks of Naproxen in Older Adults

What Can Happen

Naproxen can irritate the stomach and intestines. In adults over 65, this may lead to:

Stomach pain or indigestion

Heartburn or nausea

Stomach or intestinal ulcers

Bleeding in the digestive tract

Sometimes bleeding can occur without obvious pain, making it harder to detect early.

Higher Risk If You:

Have a history of ulcers or stomach bleeding

Take blood thinners, aspirin, or steroids

Drink alcohol regularly

Use naproxen daily or at high doses

Warning Signs to Watch For

Black or tarry stools

Vomiting blood or material that looks like coffee grounds

Ongoing stomach pain or weakness

These symptoms can be serious. If they appear, seek medical care right away.

Kidney Risks of Naproxen After 65

Why Kidneys Are Affected

The kidneys rely on prostaglandins to maintain healthy blood flow. Naproxen can reduce this flow, which may:

Lower kidney function temporarily or permanently

Cause fluid retention and swelling

Raise blood pressure

Have chronic kidney disease

Have heart failure or liver disease

Are dehydrated

Take diuretics ("water pills") or blood pressure medications like ACE inhibitors

Safe Use Tips for Naproxen Over 65

Many adults over 65 use naproxen safely by following a few key principles.

Use the Lowest Effective Dose

Start with the smallest dose that relieves pain

Avoid increasing the dose without medical advice

Do not combine with other NSAIDs

Limit Duration

Short-term use is generally safer than long-term use

Chronic daily use should be monitored by a doctor

Monitoring: A Smart Safety Step

If naproxen is part of your regular routine, your doctor may recommend:

Periodic kidney function blood tests

Blood pressure monitoring

Checking for anemia (which can signal hidden bleeding)

These checks help catch problems early, often before symptoms appear.

Alternatives to Naproxen

Depending on your health history, your doctor may suggest other pain strategies, such as:

Acetaminophen (safer for the stomach and kidneys when used correctly)

Topical anti-inflammatory gels or creams

Physical therapy or guided exercise

Heat or cold therapy

Short courses of naproxen with stomach-protective medication

Never switch or stop medications without professional guidance.

When Naproxen May Not Be Appropriate

In some situations, naproxen may not be the right choice, especially without close supervision. This includes:

Active stomach ulcers

Advanced kidney disease

Uncontrolled heart failure

Recent gastrointestinal bleeding

In these cases, a doctor can help weigh risks and benefits and suggest safer alternatives.

When to Speak to a Doctor Right Away

You should speak to a doctor immediately or seek urgent care if you experience:

Severe stomach pain or bleeding

Sudden swelling or shortness of breath

Very low urine output

Confusion or extreme fatigue

Chest pain or stroke-like symptoms

These can be life-threatening and require prompt medical attention.

The Bottom Line

Naproxen can be an effective pain reliever for adults over 65, but it deserves respect. The stomach and kidneys are more vulnerable with age, and risks increase with higher doses, longer use, and certain health conditions.
