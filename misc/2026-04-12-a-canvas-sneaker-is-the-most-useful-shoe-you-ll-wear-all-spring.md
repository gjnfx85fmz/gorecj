That said, there are some nuances to men's canvas shoes that you need to consider. First off, you can choose between laced and slip-on shoes. Both work equally well from a style perspective, but laceless shoes are really having a moment right now, given their easy-to-wear fit.

What to Consider

Laces

Most canvas shoes come with a traditional lace design, which is two separate panels on either side of the tongue. This leaves about a one inch gap covered by the laces, allowing for the shoe to open wide to get the foot in and out. The only drawback is that this lace style has to be tied every time. A split panel lace design has two panels that touch, held closed by a much shorter shoelace. This design can be laced loosely, allowing the shoe to function like a slip-on that can be tightened when needed.Then there are slip on canvas sneakers that don’t have any laces. These are the easiest to wear and provide a sleeker silhouette but the drawback is that without laces, the shoe isn’t as secure.

Cushioning

Most canvas shoes don’t have a real midsole, so all of the cushioning comes from the insole. Look for an insole that has some type of padding in it, usually foam. Some shoes have an upgraded memory foam or EVA material that will adjust to your foot and provide enhanced support. Removable inserts are always helpful because you can replace them when they wear out or upgrade them with orthopedic inserts.

Outsole

A good outsole is required for sure footing. Most canvas shoes have a textured rubber outsole. Vulcanized rubber is harder and more durable. Gum rubber is softer, which provides more grip, but will also wear out faster. Regardless of what the outsole is made of, check for a good texture on the bottom that can handle wet spots.
