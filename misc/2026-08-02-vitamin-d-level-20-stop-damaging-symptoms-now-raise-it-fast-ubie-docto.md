Vitamin D Level of 20: Symptoms and How to Increase It

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

A vitamin D level of 20 ng/mL is considered low, sitting at the border between deficiency and insufficiency. This range can contribute to fatigue, muscle weakness or cramps, bone and back pain, frequent infections, and low mood.

You can raise your level safely through midday sun exposure, vitamin D-rich foods (like fatty fish, egg yolks, and fortified dairy), and supplements—typically 800 to 2,000 IU daily. Higher short-term doses should only be used under medical supervision, with a recheck in 8 to 12 weeks. Underlying conditions, absorption issues, and red-flag symptoms may require prompt medical evaluation.

Because low vitamin D symptoms overlap with many other conditions—thyroid issues, anemia, depression, and more—it's worth clarifying what's actually driving how you feel before choosing a treatment path. Explanation

If your blood test shows a Low Vitamin D 20 ng/mL, you're not alone. Vitamin D deficiency is very common worldwide. A level of 20 ng/mL (nanograms per milliliter) is generally considered insufficient by most medical guidelines, and some experts classify it as deficient.

Vitamin D plays a crucial role in bone strength, immune function, muscle health, and mood regulation. While a level of 20 ng/mL is not usually an emergency, it is low enough to cause symptoms and long-term health risks if left untreated.

Let's break down what this means, the symptoms to watch for, and how to safely increase your vitamin D levels.

What Does a Low Vitamin D 20 ng/mL Mean?

Most major health organizations define vitamin D levels as:

Below 20 ng/mL – Deficient

20–29 ng/mL – Insufficient

30–50 ng/mL – Adequate for most people

Above 100 ng/mL – Potentially toxic

If your result shows Low Vitamin D 20 ng/mL, you are right at the borderline between deficiency and insufficiency. This level may not cause obvious symptoms in everyone, but it can affect your body in subtle and sometimes significant ways.

Vitamin D helps your body absorb calcium and phosphorus, which are essential for strong bones and teeth. Without enough vitamin D, your body struggles to maintain bone density and muscle strength.

Why Is Vitamin D Important?

Vitamin D is more than just a "bone vitamin." It affects multiple systems in your body.

1. Bone Health

Vitamin D allows your body to absorb calcium properly. Without it, even if you consume enough calcium, your body cannot use it effectively.

Long-term deficiency increases the risk of:

Osteoporosis

Bone fractures

Bone pain

2. Muscle Function

Low Vitamin D 20 ng/mL is associated with muscle weakness. This can increase the risk of falls, especially in older adults.

Seasonal depression

What Causes a Low Vitamin D 20 ng/mL?

Several factors can lead to low vitamin D levels:

Limited Sun Exposure

Your skin produces vitamin D when exposed to sunlight. Risk increases if you:

Spend most of your time indoors

Live in northern climates

Wear sunscreen consistently (which is important for skin cancer prevention)

Cover most of your skin for cultural or medical reasons

Skin Tone

People with darker skin need more sun exposure to produce the same amount of vitamin D as those with lighter skin.

Age

As you age, your skin becomes less efficient at producing vitamin D.

Diet

Vitamin D is found in limited foods, such as:

Fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines)

Fortified milk and cereals

Egg yolks

Diet alone is often not enough to correct a deficiency.

Medical Conditions

Certain conditions reduce vitamin D absorption, including:

Celiac disease

Crohn's disease

Chronic kidney disease

Liver disease

If you have one of these conditions and a Low Vitamin D 20 ng/mL, you should speak with your doctor about a personalized treatment plan.

How to Increase a Low Vitamin D 20 ng/mL

The good news is that improving your vitamin D level is usually straightforward.

1. Safe Sun Exposure

Short periods of sun exposure can help:

10–30 minutes of midday sun

Several times per week

Arms and legs exposed when possible

However, balance is key. Avoid sunburn and follow skin cancer prevention guidelines.

2. Vitamin D-Rich Foods

While food alone may not fully correct a Low Vitamin D 20 ng/mL, it helps support treatment.

Add:

Fatty fish (2–3 times per week)

Fortified dairy or plant milk

Fortified cereals

3. Vitamin D Supplements

For many people, supplements are the most effective solution.

Typical recommendations (but always confirm with your doctor):

800–2,000 IU daily for mild deficiency

Higher doses (such as 5,000 IU daily or weekly high-dose therapy) may be prescribed for more significant deficiency

Your doctor may recheck your blood levels after 8–12 weeks to ensure improvement.

How Long Does It Take to Improve?

With proper supplementation:

Levels often improve within 2–3 months.

Symptoms such as fatigue and muscle weakness may gradually improve.

Bone density improvement takes longer.

Consistency matters more than speed.

When Is Low Vitamin D Serious?

A Low Vitamin D 20 ng/mL is not usually life-threatening. However, it can become serious if:

Levels continue to drop

You develop bone pain or fractures

You experience severe muscle weakness

You have underlying kidney or liver disease

Seek medical care promptly if you experience:

Severe bone pain

Difficulty walking

Frequent fractures

Symptoms of high calcium (after starting supplements), such as vomiting or confusion
