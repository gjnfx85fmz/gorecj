What Is Altitude Sickness?

You may hear altitude sickness referred to as AMS or acute mountain sickness, which can happen when you ascend to higher altitudes too fast. When you reach high altitudes, the oxygen and air pressure levels are much lower, which can cause a range of symptoms as your body scrambles to adjust to less oxygen.

Common altitude sickness symptoms include nausea, headache, dizziness, vomiting, challenges sleeping, fatigue, rapid heart rate, and shortness of breath. Your symptoms can be mild to severe, and in some instances, they can be life-threatening.

Generally, altitude sickness starts to happen when you get above 8,000 feet, but it can also impact people at lower elevations. How severe your altitude sickness is can depend on various factors, including the altitude levels you reach, the ascent rate, genetic factors, and your fitness level.

Suppose you have plans to travel to an area that is at a higher altitude. In that case, you must take precautions to prevent altitude sickness, including staying well-hydrated, acclimatizing slowly, avoiding sleeping pills or alcohol, and taking vitamins if your doctor recommends it.
