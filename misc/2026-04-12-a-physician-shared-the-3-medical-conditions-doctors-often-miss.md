# A Physician Shared the 3 Medical Conditions Doctors Often Miss

Internet celebrity doctor Doctor Mike Varshavski, a.k.a Dr. Mike, has gained millions of fans with his medical know-how and detailed explanations of complex health issues. But even the doctor knows medicine—and medical professionals—aren’t without fault.

[the Medical](https://peplawllc.com/blog/2864537991.html)

In a new video on his ever-popular YouTube channel, Dr. Mike detailed three health problems doctors often miss. But the reason they miss them isn’t necessarily negligence. Rather, it’s based on a basic concept doctors learn in medical school: “When you hear hoofbeats, think horses, not zebras.”

[Doctors Often Miss](https://mindpeptide.bbsxyxy.com/blog/2245083027.html)

Dr. Mike explains the saying is meant to encourage medical students to think in terms of differential diagnosis, which is the process of listing illnesses in most probable to least probable order based on symptoms. In doing this, a doctor is more likely to treat the ailment early on rather than later.

This line of thinking, however, isn’t entirely foolproof—especially when it comes to the three illnesses Dr. Mike outlines. As the internet-famous doctor explains ESD or Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, PCOS or polycystic ovary syndrome and thyroid disease are among the illnesses doctors often miss. Why? Well, they are zebras.

“I admit that we in the medical community forget about our zebras—our patients with these rare, chronic, incurable illnesses that go undiagnosed for decades at a time simply because we are thinking horses, not zebras,” Dr. Mike says in the video.

[Shared the Medical Conditions](https://innate-signal.jcesqc8.com/blog/4376329575.html)

As Dr. Mike outlines, PCOS can be often ruled as a simple case of bad acne, though it is estimated to impact 8 to 20 percent of reproductive-age women. Thyroid disease similarly impacts about 12 percent of the population at some point in their lifetime, yet is often dismissed as depression or anxiety or early menopause symptoms. Dr. Mike says up to 60 percent of those with thyroid disease don’t even know they have it.

Then there’s Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, a connective tissue condition characterized by hyper-mobility of joints, “elastic” skin, and weak muscles. EDS has gained increased attention in recent years, thanks to disability activists. But the condition can be misdiagnosed as easy bruising or simple dislocation of joints. However, there’s so much more to EDS that can be addressed or mitigated with medical intervention, making diagnosis essential. Only about 1 in 5,000 people have EDS, but those who do can experience life-threatening symptoms in severe cases.

Dr. Mike says he encourages patients to advocate for themselves if they have some symptoms of these three conditions, yet can't get a solid diagnosis from a doctor. And he encourages doctors to take time to consider rare conditions when working with patients.

After all, hoofbeats may make most people think of horses — but don't be shocked when a pack of zebras shows up instead.
