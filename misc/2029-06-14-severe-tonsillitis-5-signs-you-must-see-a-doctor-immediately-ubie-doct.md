When should you see a doctor for recurrent or severe tonsillitis?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Recurrent or severe tonsillitis requires medical attention if you experience frequent sore throats (five or more episodes per year), difficulty breathing or swallowing, high fever, swollen lymph nodes, or symptoms that don't improve with standard treatments like rest, fluids, or antibiotics. Persistent tonsillitis may lead to complications such as abscesses, sleep apnea, or the spread of infection, so timely evaluation by a healthcare provider is essential to determine whether further treatment—such as a tonsillectomy—is needed.

Explanation

Recurrent or severe tonsillitis can be a troublesome condition, especially if it happens often or causes significant discomfort. When to See a Doctor: 1. Frequent Sore Throats: If you or your child have multiple sore throats in a year, it might be time to visit a doctor. 2. Breathing Problems: If tonsillitis is causing trouble with breathing, it's important to get medical help. 3. No Improvement: If usual treatments like rest and fluids don't help, a doctor should be consulted. Treatment Options: Doctors may suggest different treatments depending on how often and how severe the tonsillitis is. In some cases, they might recommend a tonsillectomy, which is a surgery to remove the tonsils. This is more common if tonsillitis keeps coming back or causes other problems. Conclusion: It's important to see a doctor if tonsillitis is affecting your daily life.
