Doctor's Note: Is Ozempic Causing Hyperpigmentation or "Ozempic Face"? A Doctor's Honest Guide

Ozempic (semaglutide) has become a household name in weight management and type 2 diabetes care. Alongside its benefits, questions have emerged about skin changes—especially hyperpigmentation or the so-called "Ozempic Face." This guide breaks down what we know, what remains unproven, and how to care for your skin, with special attention to Ozempic and melasma in Black skin.

Slow stomach emptying

Improve blood sugar control

These effects lead to weight loss and better glucose levels—but rapid changes can also affect your skin's appearance.

Understanding "Ozempic Face"

Many describe "Ozempic Face" as a gaunt, hollow, or aged look after losing weight. This is not true hyperpigmentation, but rather:

Loss of facial fat

Thinning skin

More pronounced wrinkles and hollows

Key points:

It's a mechanical change, not a change in skin color

More evident if weight loss is rapid (over 1–2 kg per week)

Can be managed with good nutrition, facial exercises, and possibly dermatologic treatments

Clinical trials and post-marketing reports list skin reactions such as rash, itching, or injection-site redness—but not dark spots or melasma.

Possible Contributing Factors (Not Proven Causes)

While Ozempic itself isn't known to trigger melasma, some related factors might play a role:

Sun Sensitivity• Rapid weight loss can change your body's metabolism, potentially increasing sun sensitivity.• Unprotected sun exposure worsens all hyperpigmentation.

Nutritional Changes• Reduced calorie intake may lead to lower levels of vitamins C and E, both vital for skin health.• Deficiencies can impair skin repair and pigment regulation.

Inflammation or Irritation• Some people experience mild inflammation at injection sites or skin flares (e.g., eczema).• Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation can arise in darker skin after any skin injury or irritation.

Hormonal Fluctuations• Weight loss can slightly shift hormone levels, but not to the degree seen in pregnancy or hormone therapy.

Practical Skin-Care Tips for Black Skin on Ozempic

Even if Ozempic isn't a direct cause of melasma, taking good care of your skin can prevent and manage dark spots:

Sun Protection

Use broad-spectrum SPF 30+ daily

Wear a wide-brimmed hat and UV-blocking sunglasses

Reapply sunscreen every two hours outdoors

Topical Treatments

Vitamin C serums to brighten and neutralize free radicals

Niacinamide to calm inflammation and reduce pigment transfer

Hydroquinone or azelaic acid (under a doctor's guidance) for stubborn areas

Gentle Cleansing and Moisturizing

Avoid harsh scrubs or exfoliants that can trigger post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation

Opt for mild, fragrance-free cleansers and rich moisturizers

Serious Side Effects of Ozempic: A Quick Reminder

While skin changes are typically mild, Ozempic can cause serious reactions in rare cases. Seek immediate medical attention if you experience:

Severe abdominal pain (–> risk of pancreatitis)

Fast or irregular heartbeat

Signs of an allergic reaction (hives, swelling of face or throat, difficulty breathing)

Persistent, severe nausea or vomiting

Always speak to a doctor about any new, severe, or persistent symptoms.
