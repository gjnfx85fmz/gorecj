What is the difference between being overweight and obese?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Overweight and obesity both refer to having excess body fat, but they differ in degree. Overweight is defined as a Body Mass Index (BMI) of 25 to 29.9, while obesity is more severe, with a BMI of 30 or higher. Obesity carries a greater risk of health complications, including heart disease, type 2 diabetes, and high blood pressure.

Explanation

Being overweight and being obese are terms used to describe having more body fat than is considered healthy. The main difference between the two is the amount of body fat.

Overweight: This is when a person has more body fat than is considered normal but not as much as someone who is obese.

Obese: This is when a person has a lot more body fat, which can lead to more health problems.

Researchers use different ways to measure body fat, such as body mass index (BMI), to decide if someone is overweight or obese. BMI is a number calculated from a person's weight and height. According to the references, there are specific BMI ranges that help classify if someone is overweight or obese, with obesity having a higher BMI than being overweight.

Understanding these differences is important because being overweight or obese can affect health in different ways.
