Explanation

Aspirin desensitization is a structured medical approach often used for patients with nasal polyps who also have aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (AERD). Under the care of an ENT specialist, this protocol can help reduce polyp recurrence, improve breathing, and lessen the need for systemic steroids. Below, you'll find a clear, step-by-step guide to what the process involves, who it's for, and what to expect. Always speak to a qualified ENT doctor about any serious or potentially life-threatening concerns.

What Is Aspirin Desensitization?

[Act Now Ubie Doctor's](https://musclemend.sunsimiaosd.com/blog/5785236379.html)

Aspirin desensitization is a controlled procedure that gradually introduces aspirin to the body in increasing doses. Over a period of days, the immune system becomes "tolerant," so that the standard daily dose of aspirin can be used safely. For patients with nasal polyps and AERD, this can:

Decrease nasal polyp size and symptoms

Reduce sinus infections

Lower reliance on steroids

Improve overall quality of life

The process must be performed in a specialist clinic with immediate access to emergency treatment.

Who Is a Candidate?

Not every patient with nasal polyps needs aspirin desensitization. Typical candidates:

Have confirmed AERD (asthma, nasal polyps, and sensitivity to aspirin/NSAIDs)

Experience frequent polyp regrowth despite surgery

Require multiple courses of systemic steroids per year

Are motivated to adhere to daily aspirin therapy

Contraindications include active gastrointestinal bleeding, significant kidney disease, or other bleeding risks. Your ENT will review your medical history, current medications, and overall health before recommending desensitization.

Continue regular asthma and nasal spray medications

The Desensitization Protocol

Under close medical supervision, most ENT clinics follow a similar stepwise approach over 2–3 days:

Day 1

[Protocol Urgent ENT](https://lumina-dermis.sunsimiaosd.com/)

Start with a very low aspirin dose (e.g., 20–40 mg)

Observe for 1–2 hours

If tolerated, give the next higher dose (e.g., 75 mg)

Repeat observation and dosing until a cumulative dose of ~150–325 mg

Day 2 (if needed)

Continue from the last tolerated dose

Increase in small increments (e.g., 75–100 mg)

Target a cumulative dose of 325 mg by day's end

Day 3 (confirmation)

Administer a full 325 mg dose

Monitor for any late reactions

Key points:

Doses are given orally, usually under nurse supervision

Vital signs and lung function are checked before and after each dose

Emergency medications (bronchodilators, epinephrine) must be immediately available

Monitoring During Protocol

Close monitoring is essential to ensure safety:

Lung Function Tests: Before and after each dose to detect airway changes

Symptom Assessment: Watch for cough, wheezing, nasal congestion, or flushing

Vital Signs: Blood pressure, heart rate, and oxygen saturation

Minor symptoms are common and expected. If moderate or severe reactions occur (e.g., marked drop in lung function, severe wheezing), the dose is halted, and treatment is given. Most patients complete the protocol within 48–72 hours.

Post-Protocol Maintenance

Once desensitization is confirmed, maintaining a daily aspirin dose is crucial. Typical maintenance regimens:

325 mg daily for the first 6 months

Some specialists reduce to 81–160 mg daily based on symptoms and tolerance

Patients should:

Take aspirin at the same time each day, with food to reduce stomach upset

Continue asthma and nasal spray medications as prescribed

Avoid other NSAIDs or painkillers that could interfere with tolerance

Regular follow-up visits (every 3–6 months) allow your ENT to:

Check nasal endoscopy for polyp recurrence

Assess asthma control

Benefits and Potential Risks

Understanding both sides can help you weigh whether this protocol is right for you.

Benefits

Fewer polyp surgeries and steroid courses

Improved nasal breathing and sense of smell

Better asthma control

Potential Risks

Gastrointestinal irritation or bleeding

Temporary worsening of asthma or nasal symptoms

Rare serious allergic reactions

Your specialist will take steps to minimize risks—such as prescribing a proton pump inhibitor to protect the stomach—and will have emergency treatments on hand during desensitization.

Lifestyle and Support Tips

Stay hydrated and eat small meals with aspirin to reduce GI discomfort.

Keep rescue inhalers and nose sprays nearby.

Keep a symptoms diary: track breathing, nasal congestion, and any side effects.

Join support groups or online forums for people with AERD.

When to Seek Immediate Help

Although mild reactions are part of the protocol, you should seek emergency care or call your doctor if you experience:

Severe shortness of breath or chest tightness

Blood in stool or vomit

Sudden, severe abdominal pain

Signs of anaphylaxis (hives, swelling of lips/tongue, difficulty swallowing)

Final Thoughts

Aspirin desensitization protocol for nasal polyps is a proven strategy to reduce polyp recurrence and improve respiratory health in AERD patients. It requires commitment, close medical supervision, and ongoing maintenance, but the benefits can be life-changing.

Always discuss any serious or life-threatening symptoms with your doctor or seek immediate emergency care. Remember, this information is educational and does not replace personalized medical advice. Discuss the risks, benefits, and logistics of aspirin desensitization with your ENT specialist to determine if it's the right path for you.
